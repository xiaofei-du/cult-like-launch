# How to Launch Your Business by Starting a Cult Like a White Woman

Deliver a **Launch Playbook** connecting the business and the people promoting it to a following, purchase beliefs, usable campaign materials, and a publication sequence. When the user requests a specific refinement, update the affected parts without regenerating the whole playbook.

## 1. Understand the business, the user, and the audience

Start from the conversation and available material. Establish the business's nature and stage, what it actually delivers, its target audience (TA), the purchaser versus user, the desired outcome, price and purchase unit, current scope versus planned additions, and the intended audience action. Establish available channels, following, results, access conditions, and timing as relevant.

Ask about **the person using this skill**, not an assumed founder:

> What is your role in this business or promotion? How does your background connect you to this audience? What experiences, circumstances, beliefs, or concerns do you share with them, and why did you choose to serve or reach them?

They may be a promoter, employee, agency representative, affiliate, founder, or someone else. Establish whose account and voice the content will use. If the public-facing person differs from the user, distinguish their backgrounds, audience relationships, and evidence. The source's **leader** is a role in the following, not a synonym for founder or skill user. Do not transfer one person's story, identity, results, or first-person experience to another.

Use [business-discovery.md](references/business-discovery.md) when the brief is incomplete or the user's role, audience connection, or product evidence needs investigation. Reuse answered questions, ask the consequential gaps early, and continue supported work. A complete questionnaire or an approval checkpoint is not required before drafting.

Current explicit facts take precedence over older assumptions. Keep product completion separate from sales availability. When the user changes a core fact, revise the affected audience, outcome, proof, offer, and copy together.

## 2. Inspect the offer before fixing its claims

Read or inspect the available product, PDF, demo, service material, or other relevant evidence before making firm benefit claims. Extract a short chain:

**Concrete content or capability → audience burden addressed or attainable change → purchase belief supported → material that can demonstrate it.**

Separate a feature, the work invested, a product demonstration, and a demonstrated customer result. They support different claims. A demonstration may show how the offer helps; it does not establish that customers already achieved the promised outcome. For an unfinished or inaccessible product, use the confirmed brief, keep claims provisional, and identify the specific evidence needed.

## 3. Diagnose seven foundations: two beliefs and five mechanisms

First define **one customer outcome** within the offer's actual scope. Both purchase beliefs must refer to that same outcome. Diagnose separately what makes the audience doubt that they can achieve it and what makes them doubt that this person or offer can help. Consider how content could weaken either belief as well as strengthen it. (2, 11)

| Foundation | Diagnostic question and required application |
| --- | --- |
| 1. I believe I can achieve this outcome. | What makes the outcome feel possible for someone in the audience's actual circumstances? Express the belief in their language and identify the material that supports it. |
| 2. I believe you can help me achieve this outcome. | Who does "you" mean here, and why would the audience trust that person and offer to help them? Distinguish identification from evidence of ability. |
| 3. A Belief System | What shared stance goes beyond a feature description, and how does it connect to the public-facing person's real experiences or reasons for participating? (3–4) |
| 4. A Common Enemy | What does this group oppose or want to escape? Ground the user's chosen enemy in lived situations; a label such as anxiety needs the circumstances that make it recognizable. (5–6) |
| 5. A Charismatic Leader | Why do people relate to this person, pay attention to their personality, and aspire to what they represent? Use the actual audience relationship, not the person's job title. (7, 11–18) |
| 6. Built-in Evangelism | Who shares with whom, why does sharing express identity or support, and what exactly can they pass on? Supply usable sharing copy. (8, 11–12) |
| 7. Exclusivity | Who receives what access, under which conditions, and why would they want that access and tell others? Establish actual conditions or label a context-based proposal. (10) |

For each foundation, record the **current conclusion, supporting material, gap to resolve, and corresponding action or content**. Keep this compact. Mark conclusions as **Supported**, **Proposed**, or **Missing**; specify what supports them. Supported means grounded in available material, not proof that an audience holds the belief. Where audience response exists, distinguish it from the team's hypothesis. These are diagnostic foundations, not validated numerical scores or launch thresholds.

Retain the source's discussion of appearance, ethnicity, gender, motherhood, religion, life circumstances, and familiarity where relevant to the supplied backgrounds. Do not infer identity from names or photographs, assume identification from a demographic match, or copy another leader's conditions. The public-facing person need not be the target customer: identify a real connection without inventing shared lived experience. Expertise can support ability to help; it does not itself establish a following.

For unresolved exclusivity, state **To be decided** and suggest a reviewable option only where the business provides a basis. Audience targeting and gradual product releases do not themselves establish restricted access. Do not default to a fixed opening window, countdown, or numerical capacity; the source prescribes none. Keep unconfirmed conditions out of publishable copy.

## 4. Connect the core following to broader demand

Identify the **core audience** who may see themselves in the public-facing person and want or need that person to succeed. Separately identify **broader buyers** who want the result even without that personal identification. These groups can overlap, and broader does not mean everyone. (6, 8–14, 17, 19)

Use the source's progression:

**Shared beliefs and common enemy → core following and evangelism → showcase a result broader buyers want → expand participation and purchasing.**

Locate the business's current position using actual material: a proposed connection, observed ongoing support and sharing, or results available for broader reach. Written beliefs alone do not constitute a following. Plan the next relationship-building or sharing action as well as the next post. Use actual results when available; otherwise specify the demonstration that can be made now and the outcome evidence still missing. Fit access arrangements to this sequence. There is no required follower count, fixed calendar, or ban on selling while building the following.

## 5. Write and deliver the Launch Playbook

Follow [playbook-output.md](references/playbook-output.md). Establish the compact proposition and diagnosis before expanding into full copy; once the consequential inputs are sufficient, complete the supported draft without waiting for a separate sign-off. Match asset count and detail to the requested scope and channel.

Ensure both beliefs and all five mechanisms shape the work through an application or an explicit unresolved gap. Each asset should show how it supports a belief, rather than merely carrying its label. Keep internal evidence notes and proposals separate from public wording; collect remaining dependencies under **Needed / To be decided**. Attribute stories and first-person claims to the correct speaker.

Save business briefs and launch artifacts in the user's task output location. Do not add private business material or campaign examples to this reusable skill's files or shareable package.
