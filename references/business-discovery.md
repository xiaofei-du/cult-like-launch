# Business and audience discovery

Use this reference to resolve a consequential gap before committing to positioning or copy. The questions are prompts to choose from, not a form to administer in full. This is the skill's application workflow; Scrivs's theory remains in [framework.md](framework.md).

## Start from what is already known

Extract facts from the current conversation and inspect the material already available. Briefly establish the business, offer, target audience, desired outcome, and the role of the person using the skill. Ask a small number of connected questions where the answer would change the work. Do not repeat answered questions or defer every important unknown to the end of a long campaign.

| Area | What to establish | Consequential question when missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business | What it does, its background, current stage, and whether it sells a product, service, access, or something else | What exactly does the buyer receive and use? |
| Offer | Price, purchase unit, completed scope, availability, and planned additions | What is included in one purchase today, and what will be added separately later? |
| Audience | Target user and purchaser, their situation, desired outcome, constraints, and their own language | Who is deciding to buy, what are they trying to achieve, and what makes that difficult now? |
| Person using the skill | Their actual role, background, audience relationship, and reason for this promotion | How are you involved, and what connects your background, experiences, beliefs, or concerns to this audience? |
| Public voice | Whose account, name, story, and results the audience will encounter | Will you speak publicly yourself, or are you preparing material for someone else? |
| Existing connection | Current followers or community, recurring conversations, support, sharing, and trust | Who already listens or responds, and what do they respond to? |
| Evidence | Product material, examples, results, and what each can establish | What can we inspect or show that makes the proposed outcome or your ability to help believable? |
| Launch | Desired action, channel, offer controls, actual access conditions, and timing | What should someone do next, and which offer or access decisions can you make? |

Keep **product completion**, **sales availability**, and **future plans** separate. A completed product can have an unopened checkout. A planned catalogue does not mean the full catalogue exists. Use current explicit user facts when older project context suggests a different offer; identify a specific discrepancy if it matters rather than silently changing the business brief.

## Understand the person and their connection to the TA

The user may be promoting their own business, representing an employer, working for a client, or recommending someone else's product. Establish that role before writing a personal origin story or treating offer changes as decisions they can make.

Explore two distinct questions:

1. **Identification:** What shared experiences, circumstances, beliefs, concerns, or familiarity could make this audience relate to the person? Why might they want that person to succeed? What do they find aspirational?
2. **Ability to help:** What experience, work, product, demonstration, or result gives this audience a reason to trust the help offered?

A person can have evidence of ability without sharing the customer's identity. They can share a belief without having experienced the same struggle. Neither connection should be invented to fill a template. Ask what actually happened, what they believe, and why they became involved; if an origin story is unknown, draft the supported belief or product explanation and leave the personal story unresolved.

If someone else will be the public face, establish that person's connection separately. The user's background can inform the work without becoming the public speaker's biography. First-person copy must match the identified speaker and the actual scope of their involvement: promoting a product does not establish that they built it or used it themselves.

Retain the identity and familiarity dimensions discussed in the source when applicable to supplied information. Mark an unknown dimension only where it matters to the proposed connection; do not turn demographic questions into mandatory intake fields.

## Inspect the product and translate its value

Choose evidence appropriate to the offer: inspect a PDF or sample, use a demo, read service materials, or review the actual product pages. For a document, distinguish navigation, content, and usable exercises; for a service, distinguish the work delivered from the client's hoped-for result. Inspect enough to support the specific claims under consideration.

For each important claim, connect:

| Observed material | Audience burden or attainable change | Purchase belief supported | Demonstration and limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What is actually present, with a page, screen, or other reference | What the audience can do with it | Why this helps them believe the outcome is possible or the offer can help | What can be shown now, and what it does not establish |

Label the status of an inference. An organized timeline can show the order of steps; it does not measure time saved. Research effort can support the offer's provenance; it does not establish a customer transformation. A completed sample can demonstrate the approach; it does not prove that an entire future range exists.

If the material is unavailable, name the missing item and the claim it affects. Draft from confirmed facts and mark benefit hypotheses as proposals. Do not invent a demonstration, result, testimonial, personal story, access restriction, or deadline.

## Move into the playbook

Proceed once there is enough to state a defensible proposition, identify the speaker, and distinguish known facts from proposals. Complete independent material while important questions remain open. Bring forward only gaps that affect the next decision or the usability of an asset.

When the user corrects a premise, update its consequences: for example, a change from a bundle to an individual purchase affects the proposition, price copy, promised scope, and CTA. A different public speaker affects first-person wording and trust claims. Preserve settled material that the correction does not affect.
