# Launch Playbook output contract

Deliver two connected parts: **Business, following, and belief design**, followed by **Campaign materials and publication sequence**. Use the user's requested language and scope. For a full playbook, cover the material below without duplicating the same evidence in multiple tables. For a focused revision, update only the affected parts.

This is the skill's operational format for applying Scrivs's framework, not an output format prescribed by the author. The seven foundations organize his two purchase beliefs and five elements; asset counts, channels, and calendars are application choices.

## 1. Business brief and launch proposition

State what the business sells, what one purchase includes, its price if known, current scope and availability, the target user and purchaser, and the desired audience action. Identify the person using the skill, their role and connection to the audience, and whose public voice the materials use. Keep these people distinct when needed.

Express the proposition in one sentence connecting the actual offer, audience, and attainable outcome. Summarize the inspected material and the strongest supported benefit claims. Keep provisional positioning visibly provisional.

## 2. Two purchase beliefs

Use the same outcome in both audience-voice statements:

- **I believe I can achieve [outcome] because [reason grounded in available material].**
- **I believe [identified person or offer] can help me achieve [that outcome] because [reason grounded in available material].**

For each, state the principal doubt, the material that can address it, and the corresponding action or content. A shared experience, relatable presentation, or result can serve different purposes; explain the connection rather than merely attaching a belief label. Include any material mismatch that could weaken the belief. (2, 11)

## 3. Following design and current position

Define the audience layers:

| Group | Reason to pay attention | Connection and motivation | Content or action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core audience | Shared beliefs, common enemy, experiences, or familiarity | Why they identify with the public-facing person, want them to succeed, and find them aspirational | What invites a response, ongoing support, or sharing |
| Broader buyers | A result they want, including people outside the core | Why they see a useful offer even without personal identification | What demonstrates the relevant result and connects it to a purchase |

Use actual backgrounds when applying identity and familiarity from the source. Keep audience connections proposed unless available evidence supports them; neither a demographic match nor product usefulness alone establishes an existing following. (5–9, 11–18)

Give a concrete application for each original mechanism:

- **A Belief System:** Shared stance and its connection to the public-facing person's actual reasons or experiences. Product features alone do not fill this role.
- **A Common Enemy:** The user's chosen enemy, expressed through circumstances the audience recognizes and words they might use.
- **A Charismatic Leader:** The actual public-facing person, their relatable and aspirational qualities, and how their voice or presence expresses them. If no person has been established, keep that gap explicit rather than inventing a founder story.
- **Built-in Evangelism:** Who shares with whom, what identity or support the sharing expresses, and ready-to-use sharing copy. A generic instruction to share is insufficient.
- **Exclusivity:** Who gets what access under which conditions and why that opportunity matters. Separate confirmed conditions from a justified proposal or an unresolved gap. (3–10)

Across these five mechanisms and the two beliefs, include a compact diagnosis: **conclusion and status → supporting material → gap → next action or content**. Use **Supported**, **Proposed**, or **Missing** as evidence labels, not performance scores. Do not treat a proposed connection as demonstrated audience response. When response evidence exists, explain what it supports and what remains uncertain.

Locate the current position: proposed following, observed core participation, or evidence ready to attract broader buyers. Choose the next action accordingly. Core supporters and buyers may differ; no fixed follower threshold is required.

## 4. Product evidence and benefit map

Show the few mappings that matter most to the proposition:

| Actual material or capability | Audience burden addressed or attainable change | Belief supported and why | Demonstration available / evidence still needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A specific inspected item and its reference | What the audience can do with it | Achievability, ability to help, or both | What can be shown now and the limit of the claim |

Distinguish product features, effort invested, a working demonstration, and observed customer outcomes. Do not re-label one as another. If real outcome evidence is absent, use the supported demonstration and name the result evidence still needed; do not manufacture a success story or borrow figures from the source.

## 5. Campaign materials and publication sequence

Move from shared beliefs and common enemy to core support and evangelism, then to results that attract broader buyers and participation under the actual offer and access conditions. Adapt the entry point to the current position. Use supplied dates or relative ordering. (8–12, 14, 17, 19)

Cover these purposes where material supports them; a single asset can serve several, with no fixed total:

- **Belief / origin:** Express the shared stance and recognizable struggle. Use a personal story only when supplied and attributable to the speaker; otherwise write supported belief copy and identify the missing story separately.
- **Person / aspiration:** Show the person's real connection, personality, and what the audience aspires to. Use the correct speaker's voice and evidence.
- **Evangelism:** Give the core audience a reason to pass the message to someone specific and actual words they can share.
- **Result / broader appeal:** Lead with a supplied result broader buyers want, or a clearly identified product demonstration when that is what exists. Connect the material to the offer without implying unproven customer outcomes.
- **Offer / participation:** State the actual purchase unit, scope, price, and next action. Include restricted access only when established; keep proposed access arrangements in the internal plan.

For each asset provide **placement and order, intended audience, speaker, belief supported and how, complete copy, visual or material requirements, and CTA**. Combine these fields compactly when assets share a channel or speaker. If no channel is specified, write an adaptable master version. A visual brief is not a produced visual.

Include the practical action that helps establish the proposed following, such as a relevant conversation or invitation to share an experience; writing a belief statement does not mean a community exists. Describe planned interactions without claiming they occurred or sending messages without authorization.

## 6. Needed, source, and status

List only remaining gaps that affect use, identify dependent assets, and say what would resolve each. Keep unsupported personal stories, claims, results, purchase links, or access conditions out of publishable text. Use conspicuous placeholders where an incomplete draft is useful, while completing independent assets.

End with a short theory credit: **@mrpaulscrivens (Scrivs)**, **How to Get Filthy Rich by Starting a Cult Like a White Woman**, and the [original 21-part thread](https://www.threads.com/@mrpaulscrivens/post/DdJoxz1Fb4K). This attributes the theory, not skill authorship. Keep source-section references and diagnostic notes outside individual public posts.

Report actual status: playbook drafted, materials scheduled, or materials published. Draft completion establishes neither audience response nor sales effectiveness.

## Worked example from a source image

This illustrates the author's source material, not a claim about the business being promoted:

- **Material:** Image 1's "non-techy SAHM to 3 boys" and "$500,000 in 3 weeks from a $67 product."
- **Core connection:** People who experience familiarity with that background. (3–4, 11–12)
- **Broader audience entry point:** People attracted by the claimed amount, product price, and period. (9)
- **Two beliefs:** Connect the viewer's circumstances to the possibility of the desired outcome, then explain the connection to the person offering help. (2, 11)
- **Evidence boundary:** The image alone does not establish viewers' beliefs, substantiate the financial claim, or supply the complete offer, evangelism design, and access conditions.
